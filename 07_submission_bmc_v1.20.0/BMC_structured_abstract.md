## Abstract (English)

**Background:** Immunoparalysis, exemplified by the immunosuppressed Mars1 endotype, drives 28-day sepsis mortality, yet actionable hub genes and repositionable interventions remain limited. Because the underlying antigen-presentation program is already established, we framed this as a confirmation-and-validation question rather than novel-gene discovery.

**Methods:** We re-analysed the public GSE65682 cohort (802 samples: 760 sepsis, 42 controls) with an auditable multi-omics pipeline built on a three-tier positive-anchor design. Hub genes were selected by consensus of LASSO, random-forest and univariate methods; a 30-gene immune-risk signature was constructed and externally evaluated on E-MTAB-4451 (n = 106, 52 deaths) on an independent cohort and platform. Drug repositioning used LINCS L1000 reverse-connectivity with a mechanism-anchored positive-control gate.

**Results:** Five immune hubs (CD74, HLA-DQA1, CD14, FCGR3A, HAVCR2/TIM-3) were confirmed as the Mars1 antigen-presentation program, plus one non-immune passenger, FIS1 (logFC +1.26, up-regulated). The 30-gene signature reached an external cross-platform AUC of 0.638 (95% CI 0.532–0.748), comparable to the published benchmark (0.619); within-cohort cross-validated AUC was 0.659 (optimistic). Seven immune-modulating agents were prioritised, including the positive-control glucocorticoids.

**Conclusions:** The contribution is a reproducible, fully auditable pipeline, an honest external validation that is independent in cohort and platform but not in label, and an explicit experimental blueprint — not novel hub-gene discovery. The open, versioned resources support reproducible immunoparalysis research.
