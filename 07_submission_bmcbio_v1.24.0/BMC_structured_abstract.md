## Abstract (English)

**Background:** Immunoparalysis, exemplified by the immunosuppressed Mars1 endotype, drives 28-day sepsis mortality, yet actionable hub genes and repositionable interventions remain limited. Because the underlying antigen-presentation program is already established, we framed this as a confirmation-and-validation question rather than novel-gene discovery.

**Methods:** We re-analysed the public GSE65682 cohort (802 samples: 760 sepsis, 42 controls) with an auditable multi-omics pipeline built on a three-tier positive-anchor design. Hub genes were selected by consensus of LASSO, random-forest and univariate methods; a 30-gene immune-risk signature was locked on GSE65682 and externally transported to E-MTAB-4451 (n = 106, 52 deaths) on an independent cohort and platform. Drug repositioning used LINCS L1000 reverse-connectivity with a mechanism-anchored positive-control gate.

**Results:** Five immune hubs (CD74, HLA-DQA1, CD14, FCGR3A, HAVCR2/TIM-3) confirm within-cohort the Mars1 antigen-presentation program, plus one non-immune erythroid-module gene, FIS1 (logFC +1.26, up-regulated). The locked-L1 signature, the pre-specified primary external metric, gave external transport AUC 0.585 (95% CI 0.469–0.696), whose interval includes 0.5; a pre-specified equal-weight sensitivity gave AUC 0.638 (95% CI 0.532–0.748), and an optimistic within-cohort cross-validated AUC was 0.659. Seven immune-modulating agents were annotated as hypothesis-generating repositioning candidates rather than prioritised by significance: their curated immune-response concordance lies at or below the study's own 0.84 background, no agent cleared the LINCS L1000 positive-control gate (the immunosuppressant prednisone ranked in the 3.2nd percentile), and the connectivity screen was non-discriminating.

**Conclusions:** The contribution is a reproducible, fully auditable pipeline, an honest external validation that is independent in cohort and platform but not in label (score orientation was fixed on the discovery cohort's 28-day outcomes), and an explicit experimental blueprint, not novel hub-gene discovery. The open, versioned resources support reproducible immunoparalysis research.
